# Example outputs

Real runs of `think`, with project names and personal details changed.

## 1. Quit or keep a side project (short)

```
/think should I quit the marketplace idea I haven't touched in six weeks?
```

> **Call:** Don't quit and don't build — **park it on purpose.** Write down that it's on hold,
> stop opening the code, and decide again when your main project answers the question this one
> depends on.
>
> **Why:**
> - **Sunk cost:** almost nothing is sunk (a plan doc and an empty starter project), so parking
>   it is free and quitting is free.
> - **Opportunity cost:** attention is already split across two live projects; a third cuts
>   all of them thinner.
> - **Bottlenecks:** the plan's own first step was *no code* (a curation account, 20 manual
>   searches, 20 brands contacted). None happened; a code scaffold did.
> - **Tension:** optionality says run the cheap no-code test now; opportunity cost says no new
>   project. Resolved below.
>
> **The connection:** this idea's one big risk is whether you can grow an audience without
> paid ads. Your main project needs that exact skill right now. Run the test there, where it
> pays off today. If you can't grow it organically, you've learned this idea's answer for free.
>
> **Next:** add an "on hold until mid-December" note. **Change course if:** the main project's
> audience grows steadily without ads (reopen at step 0); if not, close this one for good.

## 2. Two projects competing for the same hours (deep)

```
/think deep should I keep pushing my B2B dev tool or pause it for my consumer launch?
```

> **Problem:** Limited hours: the dev tool or the consumer launch?
>
> **Lenses:** Sunk cost (lots of finished work) · Opportunity cost (attention is scarce) ·
> Bottlenecks (what's holding the launch back) · Circle of competence (which work you drift
> toward)
>
> ### Sunk cost
> **What it asks:** Starting today, would you start this?
> **Applied:** As a cheap test, yes — it's public and on your resume. As a building project,
> no. Zero out the 300+ tests and the answer doesn't change.
> **Non-obvious insight:** the engineering is done; everything undecided is a sales question.
>
> ### Opportunity cost
> **What it asks:** What specifically doesn't happen? Is it really either/or?
> **Applied:** You already set a 21-day test with a kill line. What's left is a marketplace
> listing and ~15 buyer messages — a few hours. The expensive option is adding features.
> **Non-obvious insight:** the real choice is building vs. selling, for both projects.
>
> ### Bottlenecks
> **What it asks:** Where is work piling up?
> **Applied:** The launch is stuck at demand (3 signups) and manufacturing (no quotes), while
> the website got two redesigns in one day.
> **Non-obvious insight:** pausing the dev tool just buys more redesigns unless the site is
> frozen too.
>
> ### Circle of competence
> **What it asks:** Are you choosing work because it matters, or because you're good at it?
> **Applied:** Coding and site design are inside your circle; supplier calls, customer
> conversations and licensing are outside — and that's exactly what's being avoided.
>
> ### Connecting them
> All four agree the problem isn't *which project*; it's that hours flow to code. The one real
> tension — sunk cost says give the dev tool a fair test, opportunity cost says the launch is
> the committed bet — is settled by the deadline you already set: finish the selling test at
> low effort, stop building.
>
> ### Call
> Freeze building on both. 2–3 hrs/week on the dev tool's selling test until the deadline;
> everything else to the launch's non-code work. **Next:** request 3 supplier quotes and
> publish the listing today. **Change course if:** 2+ serious buyers by the deadline (keep
> it), or signups stay flat for 3 weeks despite promotion (demand problem).
